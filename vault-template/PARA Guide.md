# PARA: The Second Brain System

PARA is a folder system for organizing digital information, created by Tiago Forte. It stands for **Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives** — four top-level categories that cover everything.

The key insight: instead of organizing by *topic* (which creates infinite nesting and ambiguity), PARA organizes by *actionability* — how alive is this thing right now?

---

## P — Projects

**Short-term, outcome-based, time-bound.** A project has a deadline (or at least a target completion date). It's something you're actively working on and will finish.

Examples:
- Launch the new app
- Plan AI workshop event
- Edit August vlog
- Fix drone

Rule of thumb: if it doesn't have an end date, it's not a project, it's an area.

---

## A — Areas

**Long-term, ongoing, no finish line.** Areas are responsibilities you maintain indefinitely. They don't get "completed" — they get tended to.

Examples:
- Health & fitness
- Finances / accounting
- Car maintenance
- Home & apartment
- Event planning (ongoing capability)
- Content creation (ongoing)
- Airbnb hosting

Areas can contain checklists, reference docs, recurring templates — anything you need to keep a part of your life running smoothly.

---

## R — Resources (the vault uses "Research")

**Interest-based, topical, reference.** Anything you want to learn about or refer back to, but isn't an active project or area. This is your curiosity zone — ideas, research, notes on topics you find interesting.

Examples:
- AI and Humanity poem
- Notes on drone models
- App Store optimization guides
- Video editing tutorials
- Event marketing ideas
- Any topic you're exploring

The difference between Resources and Areas: Resources are *topics you're interested in*, Areas are *responsibilities you manage*.

---

## A — Archives

**Inactive items from the other three categories.** When a project finishes, an area becomes irrelevant, or a resource is no longer interesting, move it here. Archives are not "deleted" — they're a safe, searchable graveyard.

Examples:
- Completed projects moved here
- Old areas you no longer maintain
- Outdated research notes

The archive is what makes the rest of the system work: without it, your active folders get cluttered with dead weight. The rule is: **if you haven't touched it in 3 months, archive it.**

---

## How PARA Works in Practice

1. **Start every day in Projects.** That's what you're doing today.
2. **Use Areas for reference material** related to ongoing responsibilities.
3. **Drop ideas into Research** without guilt — that's what it's for.
4. **Archive ruthlessly.** When a project ships, move it. When an area goes dormant, file it.

The whole system is designed for one thing: **reducing cognitive load about where things go, so you can focus on what matters today.**
